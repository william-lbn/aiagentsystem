# v1.0 Content Audit

## Objective

Establish the public v1.0 textbook on Build System 1.1.2. The target is a systems textbook that explains AI Agents through theory, executable mechanisms, failure semantics, open-source implementations, experiments, production design, research evidence and industry consequences.

## Chapter-level invariants

All 40 chapters keep the same pedagogical spine while eliminating templated prose:

- problem and learning goals;
- four chapter-specific core concepts;
- system invariant and failure model;
- formal model + falsifiable hypothesis + suggested metrics;
- mechanism/data/control/recovery flow;
- minimal executable implementation;
- open-source/system comparison;
- normal/fault labs;
- production scenario and operational boundary;
- performance/reliability engineering;
- 2026-09-11 research update;
- Research Gap/Open Problems;
- evidence-chain audit and advanced practice.

## Anti-template checks

- 40 chapters × 4 concepts = 160 concept definitions; all 160 definitions are unique.
- Every chapter contains exactly one formal-model block and exactly one 2026-09-11 research-update block.
- No exact prose paragraph of at least 220 characters appears in three or more chapters after normalization.
- Repeated environment setup was compressed into Appendix A references; chapter bodies retain only theme-specific validation requirements.

## Cross-book additions

- Appendix E: 2026 research map, system theory, open problems and 2026–2030 structural research trends.
- Appendix F: current state, constraints, impact and future development across software/IT, finance, healthcare/life sciences, science, education, legal/professional services, manufacturing/supply chain, robotics/physical AI, cybersecurity, data/BI, retail/service/sales, public sector and creative industries.
- Appendix B is regenerated as the publishing view of 87 source locks.

## Claims discipline

The textbook distinguishes: reproducible code facts; primary-source factual claims; analytical judgments; forecasts. Industry percentages are reported with their source and sample limitations, and vendor product releases are not generalized into sector-wide ROI claims.
